The median view misses most of them

The burden map flags {{ r.flagged_tracts }} of {{ r.tracts }} tracts, where the typical household's bill passes 6%. Only {{ share(f.under_6, a.under_6) }} of the households under the line live there. The other {{ share(a.under_6 - f.under_6, a.under_6) }} live in tracts whose median looks fine, which is the trouble with judging a neighborhood by its middle household.

What this is not: a count of households paying more than 6%. ACS reports bills and incomes separately, never together, so this asks a narrower question: who earns too little for the typical local bill to stay under 6%? Lower-income homes often use less energy than their tract's median, which would lower the count. Gas bills in ACS's top bin ("$150 or more", {{ r.lines|selectattr("floor")|list|length }} tracts) are floors, which would raise it. Incomes are spread evenly within each Census income bin. DOE's LEAD tool models burden by income and tenure directly, and is the next source to bring in.
